00

Brief & diagnosis

Where we started

The site is well built. The problem is not taste — it is uniformity. One section archetype opens six sections; one surface treatment carries every block; one animation runs in five components; one accent marks every semantic.

What reads as template

One section openingSectionHeading.tsx applied six times — honey pill, centred display heading, subtitle

RectanglesEvery corner rounded, from a company named after a hexagon
What already works — protect it
  • The copy voice. “If it only works in a demo, it does not work.” Confident, specific, anti-hype. This is the brand; everything else exists to give it a body.
  • The warm ivory canvas. A real decision in a category that defaults to near-black. Do not go dark mode.
  • The hex bullet. A brand-derived glyph doing a functional job — the seed of the whole identity system.
  • The wordmark letter-converge. Alternating entry, long settle, no bounce. The physics was already right; it is now the law.
  • The founder’s question. “Six months after we hand it over, is anyone still using it?” — currently a quote in a card, actually the spine of the brand.
The strategic correction

Stripe, Linear and Vercel sell products, and their sites are possible because they can put the product on screen. Hivecrest sells services, and is early. Copying that playbook literally produces a beautiful site designed around an empty centre. The premium signal available to a young services firm is not gloss — it is specificity and proof. This system is built to carry proof well, not to substitute for it.

01

Creative direction

The Settled State

Every software company sells Day One — the launch, the reveal, the product shot. Hivecrest’s own writing measures value somewhere else entirely.

“Make the working day shorter.” “A team uses every morning.” “Six months after we hand it over, is anyone still using it?” The company’s belief is that launch day is meaningless, and the only thing that counts is an ordinary Tuesday, half a year later, when the software either holds or it doesn’t.

So the brand is not designed around the reveal. It is designed around the sixth month — proven, warm, worn-in, calm. The site opens in noise and resolves into quiet. It decrescendos: motion, density, contrast and colour temperature all reduce as the page descends.

Why this is memorable

Every site escalates toward a call-to-action crescendo. This one arrives at stillness. A visitor remembers it because no site they have ever scrolled got calmer toward the bottom — and because it is selling the same thing it is doing.

The five laws
Law 1

Design the sixth month, not the launch.

Nothing may look new, unboxed, or aspirational. It looks proven.

Violation: the About section illustrates the company with travel_vlog_video.mp4 — 13 MB of footage of somewhere else, doing something else.
Test: could this have been photographed, written or drawn on an ordinary Tuesday inside a client’s office?

Law 2

Every visible line is load-bearing.

A line separates, aligns, measures, or bounds. A line that only decorates is deleted.

Violation: the honeycomb at 5% opacity — the brand motif as wallpaper, holding nothing up. The hexagon’s real property is structural: maximum load per unit of material.
Test: delete it. Did anything become harder to read, align, group or navigate?

Law 3

Mass, not magic.

Motion obeys weight and friction. Objects settle; they never materialise.

Violation: opacity 0→1, y:32 in five components. Matter does not fade into existence.
Test: could a physical object of known weight perform this motion?

Law 4

One loud thing per screen.

Exactly one element per viewport sits at full volume. Everything else steps down hard.

Violation: every section gets a honey chip and a 60px bold heading and a subtitle — six times, at identical volume.
Test: squint until type is unreadable. If two elements still compete, one is wrong.

Law 5

Nothing is centred unless it is a monument.

Centred composition is the absence of a decision. It carries no tension and gives the eye nothing to travel along.

Violation: near-universal centring across every section.
Test: centring has a budget of two uses across the entire site.

Voice

The writing already on the site is the strongest asset the brand owns. It is codified so it cannot erode.

State, don’t sell.Describe what is true. Never claim what it will feel like.
Concrete over abstract.“Three spreadsheets and a WhatsApp group” beats “fragmented workflows”.
Short sentences carry weight.Average under 20 words. One sentence per idea.
Admit limits.“If it only works in a demo, it does not work” outperforms any superlative.
Sentence case everywhere.The only exception is the structural label style.
No exclamation marks.Ever.

Banned words: seamless · cutting-edge · empower · revolutionize · leverage · solutions-driven · best-in-class · game-changing · unlock · elevate · robust · bespoke · synergy.

02

Colour

Semantic, not decorative

Colour here is not a palette. It is a semantic system with four roles, and every value belongs to exactly one.

FamilyMeansWhere it lives
IvoryThe light of the working day. Warms as the page resolves.Every page surface
InkStructure and substance. The material things are made of.Text, rules, borders, the dark chapter
SlateThe unresolved. The problem, the before, Day Zero. Cold.The noise register only
HoneyThe mark of resolution. The thing that works. Warm.Marking. Never decoration.
Why this matters more than the values

Slate and honey are opposites in meaning, not merely in temperature. A section depicting the problem is cold; a section depicting the outcome is warm. You cannot use honey decoratively without lying — which is precisely the constraint the current site lacks.

The measured warning

honey-3 — the brand gold — scores 2.02 against ivory. honey-2 scores 1.59. Neither may ever carry text on a light ground. On this dark ground the same two score 8.94 and 11.35. Gold is a fill, not an ink — and this is the single most common way a gold brand goes wrong.

The honey budget — three marks per viewport

Honey may mark only:

  1. The single most important word or figure on a screen.
  2. The active state of a navigation item.
  3. The focus ring.
  4. The hex bullet.
  5. A resolution moment in the narrative — where a problem becomes solved.

Anything not on this list is not permitted to be honey. Measured from the current source, a single viewport routinely carries nine or more honey elements. Eyebrow labels, capability chips, stack tags, index numbers and icon tiles all move to ink. The accent recovers its meaning by being spent less.

Flat colour

Gradients are forbidden. No linear or radial gradients, no gradient text fills, no gradient borders, no glow washes. A gradient is light that has no source; it belongs to the launch-day register and is the fastest way for a gold brand to read as ceremonial rather than engineered. Two exceptions, both non-visible: mask-image and the understructure’s reveal falloff — a gradient used as a tool, never as colour.

03

Typography

Discipline, not replacement

Faces are unchanged: Space Grotesk and Inter, both already installed. The faces were never the reason the site read generic — having no scale contrast, no weight discipline and no assigned jobs was.

The two voices
VoiceTokenCarries
Structural--font-display
Space Grotesk		Display headings · all labels · all numerals · all measurements · all timestamps
Reading--font-text
Inter		Body copy · long-form paragraphs · form fields · buttons

This split solves the missing-monospace problem without adding a face. Space Grotesk’s geometric letterforms and even-width figures already read technical when set small, tracked and uppercase. Giving it the label-and-numeral job creates the engineering register the site lacks. --font-mono is a reserved, unbound token — switching it on later reassigns exactly two jobs and changes nothing else. No rule depends on it.

Numerals are architecture. 01–04, 09:40, DAY 00, MONTH 06 are set in the structural voice at h2 or larger, in ink-6 or ink-5. They are structural marks, not captions.

The ramp — eleven steps

The current site uses four sizes and tops out at 60px, which is why hierarchy reads flat. Fluid range calibrated 380 → 1440px.

Skip a step. Two type sizes adjacent in a layout must be at least two ramp steps apart. Neighbouring steps are too close to read as hierarchy, and produce the flat same-volume effect the current site has. --t-monument appears at most twice in the entire site — a third use makes both ordinary.

Weight whitelist — four weights exist

Semibold body, bold body, light weights and italic display are forbidden. Italic survives only at caption size, for a single attributed line.

Setting
  • Measure ceilings. Body 64ch · lead 48ch · statements at h2 and above 20ch. A line longer than its ceiling is a layout error.
  • The label style is the only uppercase in the system — and labels are not chips. A bare label preceded by a 24px hairline rule, which is load-bearing where the pill was not.
  • Tracking is bound to size. Never set by eye. Positive tracking exists at exactly one size; every step above body is negative.
  • Optical alignment above h1: round letterforms and hanging punctuation overhang the column edge.

Composition & grid

Load-bearing

The primary fix for repeated layouts and centred sameness. Content does not float in the middle of the page — it occupies one of five named seats.

SeatColumns (md+)Character
seat-anchor1 – 7Left, wide. The default.
seat-inset3 – 9Indented. Reads as a held breath.
seat-offset6 – 12Right. Creates counterweight.
seat-full1 – 12Full bleed. Structural sections only.
seat-monument3 – 10, centredBudget: 2 uses sitewide.
The half-offset law

Consecutive sections may not share a seat, and adjacent seats must differ by at least two columns. This produces the brick rag of a honeycomb’s offset rows — the comb expressed as layout rather than as wallpaper.

Mobile does not collapse to centred stacks. Below 900px, seats map to left-aligned full-width blocks with a varying left indent (0, 1 or 2 columns) inherited from the desktop seat. The rag survives the breakpoint — that is what stops mobile flattening into the generic single centred column.

Space is a token or it is a bug — arbitrary values are forbidden. Space within a group is always at least one full step smaller than space around the group. When related things sit as far apart as unrelated things, the eye cannot find the structure.

Vertical rhythm — the decrescendo
TokenMobileDesktopRegister
--rhythm-17296Noise. Dense, tight, uncomfortable.
--rhythm-296128Working.

The current site pads every section identically at 56/80px. Uniform density is why the page reads flat. Here density carries the narrative: rhythm may only increase down the page. A section may hold or open; it may never tighten. The page breathes out, once, over its full length.

Geometry

The form signature

One corner clipped, top-right, 30° from horizontal. The single most identifiable element in the system — it costs nothing to render, works at every size, and appears on every surface in the brand.

h = w · tan(30°) = 0.5774 · w  ·  clip-path: polygon(0 0, calc(100% − w) 0, 100% h, 100% 100%, 0 100%)

cut-1

The cut replaces border-radius. Radius is 0 everywhere. The current site runs rounded-3xl, rounded-2xl, rounded-xl and rounded-full simultaneously. The only surviving circles are human portraits, status dots and avatars.

Two implementation facts, not preferences

① clip-path removes box-shadow. A cut surface needing elevation uses filter: drop-shadow() on a wrapper — otherwise the shadow silently renders nothing. ② Nesting a cut child inside a cut parent at the same corner produces a stepped, ragged edge; the child reverts to a plain rectangle.

Hairlines — three weights, three meanings
WeightColourMeans
1pxink-7Separation within a group
1pxink-6Boundary between groups
2pxink-2Structural edge. Chapter-level only.

Hairlines run to the grid, not to the content. A rule stopping at an arbitrary text width is decoration.

The hex bullet

Promoted from one-off to system glyph, at 8px inline, 10px list, 14px feature. The only list marker in the system — discs, dashes, chevrons and check marks are removed. The comb survives in exactly three places: the layout offset, this bullet, and the understructure. In all three it holds something up. The 5%-opacity wallpaper is deleted.

Surface & material

Four, not one

.card-surface — described in its own source comment as “the one surface treatment sitewide” — is why every block currently reads as the same object.

MaterialGroundEdgeCutHolds
Paperpapernone + drop-shadowcut-3Content lifted above the page. Media, forms.
Insetink-8none, recessedcut-2Supporting detail. Specs, metadata, quotes.
Inkink-1nonecut-5The dark chapter. Full-bleed only.
Linetransparent1px ink-6cut-3Structure without weight. The default.
  • Line is the default; Paper must be earned. A surface gets elevation only when it sits at a different level of hierarchy from its surroundings. Elevation used as packaging is what makes the current site read as a template.
  • One elevation level per screen. A Paper surface may not contain another Paper surface.
  • Ink appears exactly once in the site. A second dark band halves the impact of the first.
  • Materials do not stack identities. A surface is one material.

Surfaces are opaque

No translucent grounds, no backdrop-filter. Three reasons, in order of weight:

  1. It contradicts the material system. A surface that shows what is behind it has no substance, and substance is the point.
  2. It cannot be contrast-audited. The measured ratio of text on a translucent ground depends on whatever happens to scroll behind it.
  3. It is expensive exactly where it must not be. backdrop-filter forces a full-screen composite every frame — one of the most reliable ways to drop frames on the low-cost Android hardware this company’s own copy promises to build for.

A brand that says it builds for patchy connections and low-cost Android phones cannot ship a frosted navigation bar. The positioning wrote this rule, not taste.

Sole exception: a full-screen modal scrim at ink-1 / 55%, which carries no text and covers the viewport rather than compositing a strip.

Motion

Load-bearing

Written as physics, not as an effects library. The current site has one motion; this defines a behaviour system.

The curves

--ease-settle is derived from the one piece of motion craft already on the site — the wordmark’s alternating letter convergence with a long deceleration. That physics was right. It is now the law.

Durations — distance sets duration
TokenmsUsed forTokenmsUsed for
--d-tap90Press--d-settle620Element arrival

A 4px move takes 180ms; a 200px move takes 620ms. Equal durations across unequal distances is why uniform fade-ups feel synthetic — the objects have no consistent mass.

The vocabulary — four verbs
SettleSomething already present comes to rest. Ends at zero velocity, never overshoots.
ShiftSomething present changes position or size.
RevealSomething present is uncovered — by mask, clip or scroll. It was always there.
HoldDeliberate stillness. A composed choice, and the most underused verb available.

Note what is absent: enter, appear, pop, drop in. Objects are not created by scrolling.

The banned list

Hover effects that change layoutSurfaces are not magnetic
Infinite loops outside the noise registerNothing settled repeats forever
Rhythm & budget

Adjacent sections may not share a scroll behaviour. Every section is exactly one of scrubbed (the user drives it), triggered (it settles once on entry), or static (it never moves). Two consecutive triggered sections is the uniformity failure in motion form — which is the current site precisely.

RegisterMax simultaneously moving
Noise6
Working4
Resolving2
Settled1

By the foot of the page, one thing moves at a time — and then nothing does. That is the decrescendo made mechanical.

Reduced motion

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce renders the settled end state — not a shortened animation, not a cross-fade: the final frame, immediately. This system gets that for free, because every sequence resolves to the settled state. The accessible path is also the best-looking one, which is the correct relationship between the two.

08

Interaction

Load-bearing

The site’s own headline is “the digital backbone your business grows on.” The primary interaction makes that literal.

The understructure

The load-bearing grid beneath the surface, revealed by the pointer — like x-raying the frame inside a wall.

PropertyValue
LayerSingle fixed element, beneath content, above ground
ContentHex grid, 1px stroke, ink-6, 84 × 150 cell
RevealRadial mask, 260px radius, falloff 30% → 72%
Peak opacity0.09
Updatepointermove → rAF-throttled → two custom properties on :root
  • It never repaints content. No layout, no reflow; it composites on its own layer. Cheap by construction.
  • Disabled below 1024px and on coarse pointers — and deliberately not replaced by a tap interaction, because a decorative tap target teaches the user a lie about what is clickable.
  • It reveals; it never draws attention to itself. If a visitor looks at the grid instead of the content, it is too strong. It is felt, not read.
  • It is the only cursor-driven effect in the system. No custom cursors, no followers, no magnetic buttons, no spotlight. One pointer idea, executed once.
State contracts — all five are required
StateTreatmentTiming
DefaultMaterial per §06—
HoverGround shifts one ink step. No movement, no shadow, no scale.180ms · reveal
PressGround shifts one further step + 1px optical inset90ms · press
Focus-visible2px honey-5 outline, 3px offset. honey-2 on Ink.180ms
Disabledink-5 foreground, no ground, not-allowed—

Hover changes value, never position. Surfaces are not magnetic and do not rise toward a pointer. Focus is honey because focus genuinely is the most important thing on the screen when it is present. Target floor is 44 × 44px, including inline links in dense text.

The scroll contract
  • Scroll may drive time, position and reveal. It may never drive opacity alone — matter that flickers in and out of existence as the user moves.
  • Scroll never hijacks. No forced snapping, no delayed release. Smooth scrolling smooths input; it does not take control of it.
  • Any pinned section declares its cost in viewport-heights. Total pinned distance sitewide does not exceed 250vh.
Accessibility floor — ranks above any visual rule here
  1. Every foreground/background pair appears in the §02 matrix, measured.
  2. Focus order follows visual order. No positive tabindex.
  3. Every interactive element is reachable and operable by keyboard.
  4. prefers-reduced-motion is honoured, resolving to the settled end state.
  5. Text is never rendered as an image.
  6. Colour is never the sole carrier of meaning.
  7. Every animation can be interrupted by user input.

Roadmap

Where this goes
Phase 1 — Core systemComplete · awaiting approval

Philosophy, colour, typography, composition, geometry, surface, motion, interaction. Delivered as BRAND-BIBLE.md, tokens.md, DECISIONS.md and specimen.html in hivecrest/docs/brand/.

Verified: ambiguity sweep clean · cut geometry accurate to 0.39px worst case · contrast matrix computed, not transcribed · coverage test run against five components, two gaps found and patched.

Phase 2 — Identity & componentsNext
  • Wordmark & logo — custom letterforms on the hex construction grid, lockups, clear space, SVG delivery
  • Iconography — hex-derived construction, stroke weight, terminals, lucide policy
  • Photography — the duotone grade spec, subject and crop rules, forbidden list, video weight ceiling
  • Component catalogue — anatomy and all five states for every component
  • Section archetypes — allowed formats, and the no-adjacent-repeat law
  • Do / Don’t gallery
Phase 3 — The HeroThe real test

The Hero is designed referencing only this document. Any question that requires asking a human is a hole in the system, patched before proceeding. That is the acceptance test for everything above.

Phase 4 — Build-outLater

Remaining sections against the archetype set. Tokens transcribed into @theme; .card-surface, the honey-pill pattern and the uniform fade-up retired.

Deferred, not dropped — the commercial track

Excluded from the design brief by agreement, and recorded here so it is not lost. Several of these move the business further than any visual change will, and cost close to nothing:

Domain email (the site sells business email on your own domain while using a Gmail address) · form endpoint (submissions currently fall through to a mailto that silently does nothing on many devices) · WhatsApp contact path · Open Graph image (every share currently renders a blank card) · registered address, CIN, phone · dead social icons · one real case study · asset weight (a 13 MB video and 8 MB of unreferenced files).

The order that matters

A site that looks premium but has a Gmail address and no clients reads as aspiring. A plainer site with a real domain, a real address and one real case study reads as operating. Do both — just in that order.
